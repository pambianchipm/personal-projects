## MUST-FIX

**1. The meta description ships the exact sentence §6.2 prohibits.**
> "Nothing goes out without your approval."

§6.2's PROHIBITED column: *"The same claim **without** the armed-in-advance qualifier."* This is that, verbatim, with the qualifier removed. It is also the single line Google prints under your name, so it is the most-read sentence you own. §5 of this same file writes three paragraphs about how dangerous that omission is and then §4 commits it one page earlier. Same file. Two answers. That is a law-4 fail on its own.

**2. All three heroes make an absolute approval promise the shipped product breaks.**
> Hero A: "You approve. It ships."
> Hero B: "it hands the work back to you before **any** of it goes out"
> Hero C: "The whole week lands in front of you **before a single post goes out.** You tap approve."

Your own §5 says posting can be "armed in advance for the posting slots you pick." So a single post *can* go out without landing in front of me that morning — I armed it last week. B and C are not hedged claims, they are absolutes, and an absolute that has an exception is just a false sentence with confidence. A is the same breach in shorter clothes: "runs your Meta ads" plus bare approval language is precisely ban 1. Every hero needs the qualifier or needs to stop making the promise.

**3. §3.1 prints a present-tense employee. Twice.**
> "**It asks** what actually sold. **It puts** more behind what did."

§6.2 bars "anything implying the employee is available today." §6 of this file claims: *"No present-tense employee. Every employee capability is future tense."* That claim is false about its own §3.1. Two sentences earlier you were careful — "will message you" — and then the tense slid and nobody caught it. If your absence list is wrong about the file it is attached to, I can't use it to check anything.

**4. "It puts more behind what did" reassembles the ad-cap attack inside the waitlist panel.**
§5 works hard to make me unable to think "they run my ads, I pre-approve, nothing caps it." Then §3.1 hands me a better version of it, in the reader's own voice:

> *"want me to run that again tonight?"* … "that reply **is the approval**" … "**It puts more behind what did.**"

So: it runs my ad account, a text message is the approval, and it moves budget on its own judgment of what won. Nowhere does a number appear. §6.2 bars this pairing because the spend ceiling is RULED and UNBUILT — and this is a worse version of it than the one §5 defends against, because it is illustrated, in a quote box, in the panel you want people to fall in love with.

**5. §3.1 and §5 disagree about what an approval is.**
> §3.1: "that reply **is the approval**"
> §5: "Spend **always waits for a click.**"

Is a text message a click? Pick one. Right now the file says a WhatsApp reply approves a paid boost *and* that spend always waits for a click. As the guy whose card is on file, I notice that immediately, and the honest reading is the looser one.

**6. The under-form line makes two promises you cannot currently keep.**
> "**We read every one of these.** Tell us what would make it worth hiring and **it goes on the list of what it does.**"

First half: your own §7(v) says what the form does on submit is an unbuilt item and the email path is BLOCKED on Resend. You cannot promise you read something you may not receive. Second half is worse — it tells every single person who types in that box that their request becomes a feature. I will type "TikTok" and hold you to it. Delete the second sentence outright; the first one waits until the form goes somewhere.

**7. "Get first pick" is the ordering promise you already struck once.**
`waitlist-front-door.md` killed *"you'll be first"* for being "an ordering promise an operator-set cohort field cannot honour for everyone." "Get first pick" is that promise with a nicer coat on. The defense in §3.1 — *"'First pick' is true of a list you are early on"* — is circular; joining a list is not the same as being early on it, and I have no way to know which I am. Also, "first pick" of *what*? Picking implies there is more than one Player 2 and I choose. There isn't. Two false implications in four words.

**8. "It comes with a whole marketing department" is a completeness claim wearing a hat.**
§6.2 bars "all the features you'd want." A whole marketing department does TikTok, does Google Ads, does email, does the newsletter, answers the phone. You do Instagram, Facebook and Meta ads — which `channel-readiness.md` grades honestly and which is genuinely three-for-five. This header spends that honesty. And the second half —
> "**You only ever talk to the employee.**"
— is present tense about a talking surface that, by your own §2.4, is zero-of-four shipped. There is nothing to talk to. That sentence is the most believable false thing in the document, which makes it the most dangerous.

**9. The proof strip is false about the scan, never mind the employee.**
> "The one hire you make this year that starts before you finish reading this page."

Nothing started. I read the page, and then I have to paste a URL and give you an email and wait. "Starts before you finish reading this page" is not a poetic stretch, it is a factual statement that is not true of anything on the page. And you're right to be nervous about where it attaches: under a hero about hiring, I attach it to the hire, and the hire is not built. You flagged it. The flag was correct. It does not survive.

**10. §5's own shipping sentence is written in words I do not know.**
> "or **armed in advance** for the **posting slots** you pick"

I own a bakery. "Armed" is a burglar alarm or a bomb. "Slots" is a machine at a casino. I have read that clause three times and I still could not tell you what I'd be agreeing to — and it is the clause that exists specifically to keep me from being surprised. A safety qualifier I cannot parse provides me no safety and provides you no cover. Say it the way a person says it: *set once for the times you choose.*

**11. §5 quietly downgrades the canon word.**
Canon: "Nothing spends or posts without **your click**." §5's replacement: "without **your yes**." "Click" is a specific physical act I performed. "Yes" is anything you later decide was a yes — a reply, a nod, a checkbox I didn't uncheck. You loosened the safety word inside the sentence whose whole job is to be tight. Keep "click," or say both and mean it.

## WHAT IS ACTUALLY GOOD

Genuinely — and I don't say this to be fair, I say it because it's the part worth protecting:

- **The diagnosis is right and the file lives up to it.** "Player2 is in alpha and we're letting shops in a few at a time" was the sentence that made me not trust you, and it's gone, and nothing weaselly took its place. "We're building it now, and the waitlist is who we build it with" says exactly the same thing and makes me want in instead of making me back away. That is the whole trick and you pulled it off.
- **Hero A is the answer.** It is four words, every one of them is true today, and it tells me what to do. I'd fix the kicker and ship it.
- **"You say one sentence. It brings back a week."** and **"You are already doing this job at 11pm."** are the two lines that sound like a person and not like software describing itself. Everything else in §4 should be measured against those.
- **The closing CTA is the most sellable thing in the document** because it's the one thing that's real right now and it asks for almost nothing from me.
- **You flagged the proof strip before I got to it, and you were right about why.** A gate that finds what the author already found is a gate confirming the author's nose works. Keep doing that.
- **You caught the price line as a rule breach independent of the brief, and said to carry it separately as a correction.** That is the behavior of someone who reads their own rules instead of quoting them.
- **The grep found `render.mjs:88` — a test that asserts the page still contains the dead headline.** Nobody asked you to look and it would have broken the screenshot tooling on ship day. That is the single most useful line in the file and it isn't even copy.
- **You refused to put "TikTok and Google Ads are coming" on a hero even though the rules allow it**, on the grounds that on a hero it reads as a roadmap promise. Correct, and it's the kind of judgment call the rules can't make for you.
- **§6 and §7 are honest**, and §6's absence claims check out on a grep except for the present-tense one (MUST-FIX 3). No dates, no counts, no cohort size, no competitor jab, no "unlimited." I looked for all of them.

Take alternate 1 for the H2. Then re-gate. The register is right now. It's the promises that aren't.
