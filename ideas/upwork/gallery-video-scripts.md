# Gallery Video Scripts — 60-Second Talking-Head, One Per Listing

Upwork allows one ≤90s video per listing (mp4, ≤100MB); they recommend under 60s. A
plain talking-head of you beats anything produced — on a zero-review profile, a human
face is the strongest trust signal there is. Read these once, then say them your own
way; don't recite.

## Recording tips (all three)

1. One take, phone at eye level, window light in front of you, quiet room. Done > perfect.
2. Look at the lens when you make the key promise ("I'll tell you if it's not worth it").
3. No jump cuts needed at 60s. If you flub, just restart — it's one minute.
4. First 3 seconds decide whether they keep watching: open with their problem, not your name.
5. End on the next step ("order the audit" / "message me the workflow"), then stop talking.

## 1. AI Workflow Audit (~55s)

> Ever feel like your team spends half the day on work a machine should be doing — but
> you're not sure which tool to buy, or what to automate first?
>
> That's exactly what this audit answers. Here's how it works: we get on a call, I map
> how work actually flows through your business, and a few days later you get a written
> report — your top three automation opportunities, ranked, with what each would take
> to build and roughly what it would save you.
>
> I spent my time at Meta as a business analyst building an AI automation system that
> served about thirty-five engineering teams — and now I do this full-time for
> businesses like yours. Most AI projects fail because someone automated the wrong
> thing. My whole job is finding the *right* thing.
>
> The report is yours to keep, and it's vendor-neutral — use it with me, another
> developer, or build in-house. And if I find nothing worth automating? The report
> says that too.
>
> Order the audit, and let's find out where your hours are going.

## 2. AI Automation Build (~55s)

> You already know the process that's eating your team's hours — the intake, the
> triage, the reporting, the follow-ups. This is where it gets automated.
>
> I take one workflow and make it run itself, inside the tools you already use — your
> email, your CRM, your support desk. And anywhere the output faces a customer or
> commits money, a human approves it first. Always.
>
> At Meta, I architected an automation system that served about thirty-five
> engineering teams. I do this full-time now — same patterns, your scale, without the
> enterprise timeline.
>
> Everything is fixed-scope, agreed in writing before I build, and you see a working
> version at the midpoint — not just at the end. When we're done you get
> documentation and a walkthrough, so your team runs it without me.
>
> Message me a description of the workflow, and I'll tell you exactly which tier it
> fits — before you spend anything.

## 3. AI Chatbot / Agent Build (~60s)

> Most chatbots are a generic AI wrapper: ask them about *your* return policy and
> they'll make something up — confidently.
>
> I build bots that are grounded in your actual content — your docs, your policies,
> your help center. They answer from your material, they cite where the answer came
> from, and when they don't know, they say so and hand off to a human. That failure
> behavior is designed, not left to chance — it's what separates a production bot
> from a demo.
>
> On the top tier, the bot doesn't just answer — it acts: books appointments, creates
> tickets, updates your CRM, with human approval on anything sensitive.
>
> You get it deployed live — website, Slack, or your support desk — with an accuracy
> pass on real questions, and documentation so your team can update its knowledge
> themselves. No proprietary platform, no lock-in.
>
> Send me five questions your customers actually ask, and I'll show you what a
> grounded answer looks like.
