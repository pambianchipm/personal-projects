## The 12 disciplines of a mature B2B marketing org (p12)
"A typical mature B2B mktg org spans disciplines" — 12 boxes in a 3x4 grid:

2. Digital and Social
3. Product marketing (PMM)
4. Comms/PR
5. Partner marketing
6. Content
7. Field marketing
8. Events
9. Demand gen
10. Growth
11. Creative
12. Marketing ops

## Jobs to be done, per discipline (pp13, 15)
"Each has core jobs to be done" — definitions given for all 12:
- **Brand:** "Owns brand positioning, and in partnership with creative, how a brand shows up (visuals and storytelling) in the world."
- **Digital and social:** "Manages online presence (web, email, SEO/GEO) and executes digital marketing campaigns."
- **Partner marketing:** "Leverages third parties (other brands, influencers, affiliates) to reach users. Runs co-marketing and co-branded campaigns."
- **Content:** "Produces valuable, relevant content (blogs, videos, etc) to engage targets. Drives creation of thought leadership assets."
- **Demand gen:** "Builds pipeline: generates interest in and demand for your product, and ensures sales has a steady pipeline of qualified leads."
- **Growth:** "Optimizes touchpoints across the user journey from discovery to retention. Runs experiments, has heavy PM and UX partnership."
- **Product marketing:** "Brings market and customer insights to the org. Shapes product/market fit. Positions products, enables sales."
- **Comms/PR:** "Focuses on building trust and reputation with a wide audience. Handles media strategy + engagement (and more)."
- **Field marketing:** "Facilitates local (often in person) engagement with customers. Partners with local sales team, runs local events, campaigns."
- **Events:** "Creates live and virtual experiences that engage audiences directly. Owns trade shows, customer conferences."
- **Creative:** "Ensures that the brand shows up (visually and in copy) beautifully and consistently. Copywriting, graphic design sit here."
- **Marketing ops:** "Manages the marketing tech stack (e.g. CRM, campaign tools). Owns marketing process efficiency and often analytics/performance."

## Sample evolution path for the marketing function by stage (p20)
"Here's a sample path for how the function can evolve" — table with columns Pre-seed / Seed / A (into B) / B/C/D:

**What you're doing:**
- **Pre-seed:** "Outsource to get the basics in place, DiY the strategy with a bit of expert support."
- **Seed:** "Build and execute on a basic marketing plan (hire help unless you love it/it's a good use of your time)"
- **A (into B):** "Get marketing foundations (team, strategy) solidly in place. Outsource some specialties to contain budget."
- **B/C/D:** "Add relevant specialists (order and timing varies depending on your business)."

**Staffing:**
- **Pre-seed:** Agency for core brand identity/design; light touch consultants/freelancers as needed for early brand and product positioning
- **Seed:** Marketing generalist (often part time/contract) who manages freelancers + delivers projects; freelance creative; lightweight senior advisory (esp PMM)
- **A (into B):** Head of Marketing; Director PMM; Generalist Mktg Mgr; PR agency or fractional; contract designer; contract writer
- **B/C/D:** Comms/PR; Content; Growth/Demand Gen; Field + Events; in-house creative (and more)

## Tips for navigating the reality of marketing in the startup environment (p21)
Four tips with sub-bullets (verbatim structure):
1. **Use agencies, freelancers and consultants**
   - Consultant for positioning strategy
   - Small comms agency for media engagement
   - Freelance design + copywriting for assets
2. **Go fractional for quality without the full cost**
   - Fractional CMO: two days a week
   - Runs function and team
   - No benefits cost, no equity given, minimal commitments
3. **Go senior but temporary (at key moments)**
   - Experienced mktg leader at A round to build the machine
   - Brand strategy/identity, product messaging, core team
   - 9-12 month engagement, then run it with junior team
4. **Hire marketing athletes early on**
   - True generalists exist (look mid career)
   - Hire for the best spike for you (e.g. PMM, content, comms)

# SECTION 2: Intro to Brand (pp23–33)

Section divider (p23): "Let's start with brand."

## Why brand matters in B2B buying (p24)
"Brand is highly relevant in the B2B buying process" — three stats:
- "Up to **95%** of buyers are **not** in the market at a given time. And, when they hit the buying window …" [Source 1: The 95/5 Rule: Why B2B Growth Starts Long Before The Purchase. J. Dawes. Ehrenberg-Bass Institute.]
- "more than **85%** start the buying journey with a specific list of brands to consider (a 'day one' list)." [Source 2: Google/Bain, B2B Discovery to Devotion Study, US, 2022 n=1208, conducted across B2B Buyers. Published in HBR and Marketing Dive.]
- "Over **90%** of those end up buying from that initial list." [Source 2]

## Day one list = awareness + reputation (p25)
"For your company to make it onto the day one list, they have to know that you exist. And have a positive impression of you. That's **brand awareness + brand reputation** in action."
"Impacts like this explain the oft-cited McKinsey finding that: **B2B companies with strong brands outperform weak ones by 20%**" [Source: "B2B Business Branding," March 1, 2013, mckinsey.com/capabilities/growth-marketing-and-sales/our-insights/b2b-business-branding]

## And brand matters at the early stages (p26)
Three reasons, each with two bullets:
- **For investors + valuation:**
  - Brand consistency → higher perceived mgmt quality
  - Strong brand → pricing power → higher LTV → higher valuation
- **To maximize organic demand:**
  - Engages more easily, simpler to drive earned media
  - Clear narrative means faster, cheaper path to consideration
- **To build cred with buyers:**
  - Signals legitimacy, lowers perceived risk
  - Demonstrates clarity of strategy (+ attention, care)

**Common (narrow) conception (p29):** "When someone says 'brand' they often are thinking" three columns:

## Brand, as defined by industry luminaries (p31)
Three quotes:
- "A brand is the set of expectations, memories, stories and relationships that, taken together, account for a decision to choose one product or service over another." — Seth Godin, author, entrepreneur, marketer
- "Branding adds spirit and a soul to what would otherwise be a robotic, automated, generic price-value proposition." — David Aaker, Professor Emeritus at UC Berkeley
- "Authentic brands don't emerge from marketing cubicles or advertising agencies. They emanate from everything the company does." — Howard Schultz, Former CEO of Starbucks

# SECTION 3: Brand identity (pp34–38)

Section divider (p34): "Brand identity."

## Brand identity = visual + voice (p35)
Two components:
- **Visual (Vis ID):** "Things you can see." — Logo/wordmark, Color, Illustration style, Photo/video style
- **Voice:** "Tone and personality. The way that your brand speaks in the world when it's represented in words."

## The brand book (p36)
"The core artifact is a brand book. It's useful to have one, even early. It helps you show up with polish, and saves time. It can be a living document."

Brand book contents (a 6-part template):
1. **Brand foundations:** your positioning, mission, vision, and core values.
2. **Logo + usage:** clear space rules and "What Not To Do" (e.g., don't stretch the logo).
3. **Color palette:** primary and secondary colors with HEX, RGB, and CMYK codes.
4. **Typography:** your primary heading font and your readable body font.
5. **Photography/illustration:** guidance on what style of imagery should/should not be used.
6. **Voice & Tone:** is your brand "Professional & Serious" or "Quirky & Helpful"?

## MVP brand book recommendation (p37)
"RECOMMENDATION — Have things like a website and pitch deck? Have an **MVP brand book** too." Same 6-part list repeated, with the middle three items highlighted in yellow as the MVP core: **Logo + usage, Color palette, Typography.** (I.e., the minimum viable brand book covers logo usage rules, colors with codes, and fonts.)

## Brand book: the scrappy version (p38)
Three steps:
1. **Source a template**
2. **Hire freelance or small agency for design***
3. **Get legal advice**

Footnote (verbatim): "*Unless you're a designer, don't DiY, or just AI. THIS IS NOT LEGAL ADVICE, but word on the street is that substantial human involvement matters for brand IP ownership. It definitely matters for creative quality. Know your AI tool Ts and Cs. Get legal advice to ensure you're doing it all right."

# The 10 most load-bearing ideas for a startup doing its own GTM

1. What marketing functions you need is determined by your business shape — GTM motion, audience, and competitive landscape — not by a standard org chart, and you may never need some functions at all (p17–18).
2. If your addressable audience is hundreds of accounts (dozens that matter), the game is product story, credibility, and high-touch relationship selling (PMM, thought leadership, events); if it's millions, the game is awareness, lead volume, and funnel optimization (demand gen, growth, digital) (p18).
3. Three aspects of marketing deserve founder attention before the first marketing hire: brand, product marketing, and comms/PR (p22).
4. The 95/5 rule plus day-one-list data means brand works long before the sale: up to 95% of buyers aren't in-market now, more than 85% start their buying journey with a day-one list of brands, and over 90% buy from that list — so you must be known and positively regarded before the buying window opens (p24).
5. Brand awareness + brand reputation are what get you onto the day one list, and strong B2B brands outperform weak ones by 20% (McKinsey) (pp24–25).
6. Brand pays off even at early stage: it raises perceived management quality and valuation for investors, makes earned media and organic demand cheaper, and lowers perceived risk for buyers (p26).
7. Logo ≠ brand: a logo and tagline are only elements of a brand's expression — the real question is whether you have a set of assets or a strategy, and brand should be grown "with intention and a clear plan" (pp32–33).
8. Brand reputation is composed of every touchpoint — pitch deck, website, investors, client roster, reviews, team interactions, service experience — not just visual assets (p30).
9. Brand identity = visual (logo, color, illustration, photo/video style) + voice (tone and personality), codified in a brand book; even early, ship an MVP brand book whose core is logo usage rules, a color palette with codes, and typography (pp35–37).
10. Staff marketing "fast and light" by stage: outsource/DIY at pre-seed, a part-time generalist at seed, foundations plus fractional/temporary senior leaders at Series A, specialists later — using agencies, fractional CMOs, temporary senior leaders, and mid-career "marketing athlete" generalists hired for your best spike (pp16, 20–21).
