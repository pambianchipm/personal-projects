## 1. THE HIRING FRAME — tested as a strong candidate, and adopted

Phin's hero:

> **"Come hire Player2. Put your business into co-op mode.** Just sign up
> here, connect your accounts, tell us about your brand, your products, your
> business, your competitors, and then Player2 will message you and will be
> your trusted employee."

**VERDICT: adopt the hire. Demote the co-op. Fix the verb.** Detail below,
strongest objections first, because the chief asked to be argued with rather
than agreed with.

### 1.1 The one argument for it that survives everything

The hiring frame is not strong because it is clever. It is strong because it
**moves the buyer into a different price folder before they ever see a
number.** That is positioning v1 §2's whole thesis — *"the folder decides
whether $299/mo is outrageous (vs tools) or a bargain (vs the services
band)"* — and Phin has arrived at the sentence that does it in three words.
`UNTESTED`, and the most valuable thing an interview could test.

It also needs **no comparative claim**, which is the constraint the chief set
and nothing else has satisfied. It is a positive assertion about us.

### 1.2 THE INVERSION ARGUMENT DOES NOT HOLD, AND IT IS THE CHIEF'S OWN

The chief's case: *"the competitor's own promise is 'do the work of a full
team without hiring one.' Ours inverts it directly — hire one."*

**That quote is not in our receipts.** It appears exactly once in this tree:
in the chief's 20:00 inbox entry (`inbox.md:110`). It is **not** in the
primary Holo snapshot, which is the OBSERVED authority and which outranks
every third-party or remembered figure by its own header. What the snapshot
records as Holo's own pricing-page header is:

> *"Pick your plan. Scale your output with more ads every month. Cancel
> anytime."*
> — `snapshots/2026-09-03-holo-primary.md` §1, OBSERVED

**Graded: UNSOURCED.** It may well be a real line on a page we cannot fetch
(tryholo.ai is egress-blocked from this box). It is not a receipt today, and
after the day this org has had — a 4× review-count error, a false refund term,
a citation that did not match its claim, all from intermediaries — an
un-receipted competitor quote is not permitted to be load-bearing. **Do not
build the frame's public argument on the inversion.** If Phin wants it, the
capture is one screenshot: their homepage hero, per the standing snapshot
doctrine. Filed as an action below.

**And the correction improves the position.** What their page actually sells

dollar. We sell someone who does the job. A buyer in the hiring folder never
computes cost-per-ad, which is the axis we lose on and the one Phin's own
research said not to fight on. That argument stands on OBSERVED text.

### 1.3 "HIRE" IS THE AI-EMPLOYEE CATEGORY'S STANDARD FRAME, NOT OURS

OBSERVED this shift, on their own page:

> **"Hire an AI Employee. Not Another Tool."** · *"One hire. The output of a
> team."* · *"60,000+ workspaces hired Viktor"*
> — `snapshots/2026-09-03-viktor-pricing.md` §3, OBSERVED

So *"come hire"* is not a novel move in software. **The honest claim is
narrower and still worth having: nobody is saying it to an owner-led local
shop about their marketing.** Holo sells creative volume; the services band
sells human hours; the tool band sells seats. The hiring frame is, as far as
this org can observe, **unoccupied in SMB social marketing** — and that is a
claim we can defend, unlike "nobody says this."

Say the narrow version everywhere. The wide version fails the first time a
buyer has seen a Slack AI-employee ad.

### 1.4 "CO-OP MODE" IS THE WEAK HALF AND IT CARRIES A REAL MISREAD RISK

Three problems, ordered by how much they cost:

1. **"Co-op" does not mean co-operative play to a shop owner. It means a
   cooperative** — a co-op grocery, a farm co-op, a buying co-op. *"Put your
   business into co-op mode"* is, to a retailer, a sentence about **business
   structure**. This is a new flag; positioning v1 §1 already warned that
   "backup / second player" may *"read as gaming jargon to a 52-year-old salon
   owner"*, and this is that risk plus a live competing definition. `UNTESTED`
   and cheap to test — it is a five-second-test question, not a debate.
2. **It fights the hire.** Hiring is hierarchical: you are the boss, it
   reports to you, you can fire it. Co-op is peer: you are teammates. The
   chief's Change 2 is exactly that *"hire" imports a person's expected
   reliability* — that import only works because the frame is hierarchical
   and accountable. Co-op softens the accountability the frame is being
   adopted for. **Two metaphors in two sentences, pulling opposite ways.**
3. **"Come hire" is a verb the product cannot honour.** Build **item 22**
   takes pricing and self-serve signup **off the landing** and closes the
   checkout route (`WAITLIST_ONLY`, defaulting ON). *"Come hire Player 2"*
   over a closed door is an invitation to do something the buyer cannot do.

**But co-op is not deleted, because the name is genuinely an asset.** Player 2
*is* the co-op player, and Phin's own disclosure framing — *"playing co-op is
better, because now I know where I should be crafting my creative"* (his
words, RULED 3 Sep) — is good, true and in his voice. **Demote it from hero to
the founder's line**, where it does its best work: in his campaigns aimed at
people already using a generation tool.

### 1.5 THE HIRE IMPORTS A WAGE ANCHOR — AND THE EVIDENCE SAYS THE ANCHOR
### PUSHES DOWN, NOT UP

This is the finding that most changes the pricing answer, so it sits in the
frame section where it starts.

The chief asked us to *"anchor against a part-time person's wage, which is
what 'hire' invites the buyer to compare to."* The instinct is right about
what the buyer does. **The evidence says the seller does not get to keep the
gain.** The one AI-employee product we can observe uses the hire frame and
then anchors **downward, hard**:

> *"Your whole team gets an analyst, an ops lead, and an engineer. **For the
> price of lunch.**"* · *"then $50 a month"* · Team plan card: **$100/mo**
> — `snapshots/2026-09-03-viktor-pricing.md` §2–3, OBSERVED

**So "hire" has not bought wage-level pricing for anyone we can see.** A
$299 defence built on *"cheaper than hiring someone"* is standing on an anchor

pretending otherwise.

`UNSOURCED`: the wage figure itself. This org holds **no receipt** for a

## 2. THE TWO LAYERS — the platform is what it does; the employee is who it is

Phin's structure, his words: *"we have this platform, this is what it does…
Then — we have our REAL positioning — which is that we have this employee that
will live in chats, or wherever you want it to, and that it's proactive,
touches up with you, and you can still access the platform too."*

**Adopted, with one change to the order of the employee's four properties.**

### 2.1 Layer 1 — the platform (what it does)

This is the layer that exists, and it is described, never totalled. Per
`channel-readiness.md`: it publishes to **Instagram** (image, Reel, Story),
**Facebook** (post, video), reads metrics back from both, **replies to
comments** on both, and runs **Meta ads** with a capped, human-gated
boost-the-winner. Approvals live in one queue; auto-publish only runs on slots
the owner armed themselves.

Layer 1's job in the position is **credibility, not desire.** It is the answer
to *"can it actually do anything?"* and it is answered with named platforms
and named actions, never with a count and never with "everything."

### 2.2 Layer 2 — the employee (who it is), and the one-line test

**A tool is a place you go. An employee comes to you.** That is the whole
position and it needs no competitor in the sentence.

The four properties, **reordered** — the chief's list ends with the strongest
one and I want it first:

1. **IT ASKS YOU THINGS.** *"How did that discount do?"* *"Did anyone come in
   about the reel?"* This is not conversational garnish. **It is the
   attribution mechanism.** Offline sales, walk-ins, what the customer said at
   the till — no integration on earth reaches those, and build item 29's promo
   codes plus Square/Shopify ingest reach only the half that goes through a
   till. **The asking is how we get the other half**, and it is the honest
   answer to the SMB gate's standing objection (*"your card charges me $299 to
   do data entry for software"*): a one-line reply to a colleague is not data
   entry. **This is the property to lead with**, because it is the only one of
   the four that is simultaneously (a) structural, (b) unclaimed by either
   comparable, and (c) load-bearing for *"we count sales, not signals."*
2. **IT LIVES WHERE YOU ALREADY ARE.** Chat, SMS, WhatsApp, Slack, Telegram —
   and potentially inside ChatGPT / Claude / Grok as a connected MCP surface,
   which is the cheapest "everywhere" available because `lib/mcp.ts` already
   is an MCP server and needs client scoping rather than a new channel.
3. **A REPLY IS THE APPROVAL.** No app to open. Phin's ruling, and it is the
   difference between a colleague and a dashboard with notifications.
4. **IT HANDS BACK FINISHED WORK.** You send a phone clip; it comes back cut
   and captioned; *"this look good?"*; your reply schedules it. **This is the
   demo that sells the tier.**

### 2.3 What is honestly ours, and what is table stakes

**Graded against the two primary snapshots, because a differentiator nobody
checked is a wish:**

| Property | Status against the observable field |
|---|---|
| It asks you things | **Ours.** Neither comparable's own page describes the product asking the customer a question. Holo's Learnings *tells* the owner (A36, empty state only); Viktor answers when addressed. `NOT OBSERVED` that either asks — absence from a page is not absence from a product, so this is *unclaimed*, not *impossible for them*. |

**And a downgrade the chief will not enjoy.** The 20:00 entry says the

### 2.4 THE HONESTY PROBLEM, STATED WHERE NOBODY CAN MISS IT

**Of the four properties above, zero ship today.** Email exists
(`lib/notify.ts`, Resend, one cron route gated by a `CRON_SECRET` Phin has not

is no open API and Apple's Messages for Business is approval-gated at large
brands; item 30 flags it investigate-before-promising.

**So layer 2 is a promise, and `RECEIPTS OR IT DOESN'T SHIP` binds promises
hardest.** The rule for every surface, no exceptions:

- **Every layer-2 sentence is labelled as coming, with no date**, or it does
  not appear.
- **iMessage is named nowhere** until a spike says it is reachable.
- A waitlist for a capability is honest. **A waitlist implying the capability
  exists is not.** Draft language in §6.3.

## 4. THE PRICES — a fee plus a generation allowance

**Every number in this section is blocked on the same thing, and I am saying
it once, at the top, rather than burying it in a caveat.**

> **Build item 24 — the generation cost ledger — is UNBUILT. This org cannot
> state what one image or one video costs us.** `generation_events` has four

> in the codebase is a *comment* (`lib/xai-video.ts:77`). And
> `ENTRY_WEEKLY_ALLOWANCE = {image: null, video: 4}` — **`null` is uncapped
> image generation on the cheapest tier.**
>
> **So: no margin claim, at any tier, is possible today. Every allowance
> number below is deliberately blank.** Item 31 says it in the build repo —

### 4.1 Entry (A1): HOLD $59. Do not drop to $29. Change the shape instead.

**Recommendation:** entry stays **$59/mo**, restated in the ruled shape as
**"$59/mo, including N image generations and M videos a month"**, with N and M
left blank until item 24 lands. On the item-22 waitlist page it appears
exactly as that item specifies: *"Founding rate from $59/mo when you're
invited"* — derived from `pricing.ts` (`ENTRY_CENTS = 5900`), never typed.

**Four reasons, strongest first.**

1. **$29 is the one move here that could be unbounded negative margin, and
   nobody can currently say.** With `image: null`, entry ships uncapped image
   generation against a per-image cost nobody records. **Cheaper is not safer
   while we are blind — it is riskier**, because the price cut is immediate
   and the allowance that would bound it is exactly what we cannot yet set.
   $29 *requires* the ledger more urgently than $59 does.
2. **$29 is competing on their axis, at their number, from behind.** OBSERVED:
   Holo promo **$12 / $29** for **120 / 350** creatives a month. Matching $29
   with a smaller allowance loses the only comparison that framing invites.
   And under the ruled split, generation is explicitly **not** where our value
   is — so pricing entry to win a creatives-per-dollar contest prices us on
   the axis we just decided is the metal, not the mind.
3. **Entry's job today is to sort a waitlist, not to convert a buyer.** Item
   22 closes the checkout route; the landing collects a list with one
   qualifying question. **A price on a waitlist is a filter.** $59 filters for
   owners who already accept that marketing costs money — which is exactly the
   interview pool item 22 says the list is for (*"a waitlist with no price
   cannot tell you who would pay, which is the whole point"*). A $29 list is
   longer and tells us less.
4. **The shape change is worth more than the number.** *"$59 including N
   videos"* reads as a package; *"$59"* reads as a subscription to be compared.
   The allowance line is what makes the metering split legible at the point of
   sale, and it is free to add.

**And Phin's instinct gets what it actually wants, without repricing the
product.** He wants a cheaper door. The disciplined version is a **founding-
cohort rate, not a list-price cut**: **$29/mo locked for the first invited
alpha cohort, stated as a founding rate with an end to the cohort, not to the
price.** Item 22 already ships a `cohort` field set by the operator. This
tests his number where it does the most good — on the people who take the most
risk — and never prints $29 as the value of the product. **This is my one
concession and I think it is the right one.**

**THE STRONGEST OBJECTION TO MY OWN ANSWER.** Entry is not competing with Holo
on allowances; it is competing for one glance at a card. **An owner comparing
two AI marketing subscriptions reads the big number and nothing else.** $59
against $29 is a 2× number on a page and it loses that glance whatever I think
we are selling — my reason 2 is a strategist's argument being asked to survive
a buyer's two seconds, and it will not. Worse, reason 3 is **convenient**: it
recasts an untested pricing choice as a research instrument, which is exactly
how a position starts protecting itself. If Phin is right, we learn slowly and
expensively, because a short waitlist at a high price looks identical to a bad
position for months.

**WHAT WOULD HAVE TO BE TRUE FOR ME TO BE WRONG.** Any one of:
(a) item 24 shows entry generation costs us well under ~$10/mo at realistic
volume — then $29 is safe and the volume argument wins on its own terms;
(b) waitlist signups at $59 stall while a $29 variant fills — item 22's
landing can carry that as an A/B, and it is the cheapest test in this file;
(c) interviews show owners *do* read the allowance line — then allowance-per-
dollar is the real axis and we are behind on it and must say so.

### 4.2 The employee tier (A2): WAITLISTED, NOT PRICED — settled

Phin ruled it (inbox 19:45) and I am not re-arguing it. What follows is **what
the number becomes when the tier exists** — explicitly not for any page.

**Recommendation for the eventual number: hold $299. Do not raise it, and
change the reason.**

The old defence — *"it is cheaper than human time"* — is gone with the service
tier, and §1.5 shows the replacement everyone reaches for is worse than it
looks: **the hire frame has not bought wage-level pricing for anyone
observable.** Viktor sells the same frame at $50–100/mo for a whole workspace.

**What can hold $299 is scope of work product, not the frame:**
- It **publishes to the owner's real accounts** and **spends real ad money**
  under a ceiling. Viktor at $100/mo does neither for a shop, by its own

- The nearest **sourced** band for "somebody does your paid social" is **A11:
  $500–2,000/mo** (Clutch 2025, VERIFIED with its own sampling caveat).

  §2 chose and Phin approved.
- It produces **finished video from the owner's own footage**, which is the

**THE STRONGEST OBJECTION.** *A bot is worth less than a person* — the SMB
gate's own move, and the chief flagged it. The defence above is a **scope**
argument, and scope is what a buyer discovers in month two; **in month zero
they price the category they think they are in**, and the category's
observable prices are $29 and $100. $299 for an AI that texts you may simply
read as three hundred dollars for a chatbot, and no amount of "but it spends
your ad budget" survives that first reaction if the demo does not land in
thirty seconds. **The tier is priced by its demo, not by this document.**

**AND THE FLOOR, SAID PLAINLY:** the margin at $299 is **unknown**. This tier
talks unprompted and edits video on request — an unbounded spend shape — and
item 24 is unbuilt. **Nobody in this org can say $299 is profitable.** That is
the honest state, and it is a reason to keep the tier behind a waitlist
independent of Phin's reason.

### 4.3 The gap (chief's Q3): keep it. There is no missing middle.

5× with nothing between, and I recommend **preserving it**.

Under the ruled split the tier boundary is **kind, not quantity**. A middle
tier can only be *more generations* — which re-prices the boundary as
quantity and destroys the one sentence that makes the ladder legible (*"the
platform is metered, the employee is not"*). **The pressure for a middle is
real and should be released through top-ups on generation, not a third tier**
(item 31 §3). Top-ups keep the boundary intact and let a heavy entry user
spend more without being sold a different kind of thing.

The gap also does useful work: it makes the employee feel like a **different
purchase**, not an upsell — which is the only way a hiring frame reads
correctly. You do not buy 1.5× of an employee.

### 4.4 The metering questions the chief asked, answered

**Does entry become "$X + N generations"?** Yes — §4.1.

**Does metered entry make the employee easier or harder to sell?** **Easier,
and for a reason the chief did not name.** Metering entry makes the upgrade an
**escape from counting**, not a bigger bill. The buyer's felt pain on entry
(watching a number) is cured by the tier where nothing is counted. That is a
rare and valuable structure — the usual upgrade is more of the same thing.

**The argument against it, which is real:** metering entry teaches the buyer
that *we are a company that counts things*, and then asks them to believe we
will not count the employee. To anyone burned by expiring credits, "unlimited"
is a word, not a term. **Mitigation is §3.3's: a written commitment, not an
adjective.**

**Soft-cap behaviour.** Agreed, and it is a position issue, not a UX detail:
**never block an owner mid-task.** Allow the overage, say so plainly
afterwards. A shop owner hitting a wall at 11pm is churn, and it is the exact
opposite of having an employee. Two additions:
- **Show usage, not a remaining balance** (item 31 §5). A countdown makes
  people ration; the whole loop depends on them not rationing.
- **No expiry inside a paid period** (item 31 §6). OBSERVED contrast, and it
  is why this matters: Holo's credits expire at cycle end and top-ups die at
  90 days (A34); Viktor rolls over for exactly one month. **A no-expiry
  commitment is cheap for us and buyer-visible.** Flagged: items 31 §4–6 are
  the *chief's* constraints, not Phin's words — **he has not ruled on these
  three and should.** Filed in the notice.

## 5. VIKTOR — the model, snapshotted, and whether it transfers

Full receipt: `gtm/market/competitors/snapshots/2026-09-03-viktor-pricing.md`
(raw fetch 2026-09-03T20:20:35Z, HTTP 200, byte counts and SHA-256 for both
pages, extracted text retained and hashed).

**Not a competitor. A model reference.** Viktor sells team ops/knowledge work
in Slack and Teams across 3,200+ tools. Different buyer, different job. It is
in this file because Phin named it and because it is the only **observable**
AI-employee pricing model. **It does not go in the competitor dossier set.**

### 5.1 What the snapshot corrected

The chief's read (THIRD-PARTY, from two direct competitors' blogs) was:
*credit-based, no per-seat fee, entry around $50/mo for 20k credits, credits
mapped to model usage.* **Three of four hold on primary capture. The fourth is
off by 2×:** `$50/mo` is a *from*-price in their `<title>`, meta description
and footer CTA; **the only plan card the page prints is `$100 / month` for
`40,000 credits monthly`.** The `20,000` figure appears once — in an unpriced
FAQ question. Both figures imply the same $2.50/1,000 rate, which is the only
support the blog claim has. **A buyer landing on that page sees $100.**

That is the snapshot doctrine paying for itself twice in two days.

### 5.2 The model, as printed

No seats (*"workspace-based. Your team shares the same workspace credits"*).
Rollover for one month; free/bonus credits never expire. **No feature tiering
at all** — *"Every plan includes all integrations, all capabilities… The only
difference is how many credits you get."* **Everything is metered**, including
scheduled automations (*"Frequency matters"*). Published task costs: quick
100–300, complex 500–1,500, projects 2,000–5,000. Free tier: $100 of credits,
no card.

### 5.3 Does "land cheap, expand on usage" transfer to an owner-led shop?

**Mostly no — and their own page contains the sentence that kills it.**

1. **The expansion engine does not exist here.** Viktor expands because a
   *workspace* expands: more people delegating more work (*"shared across your
   whole workspace"*, *"one hire can support every department"*). **An
   owner-led shop is one person.** There is no second delegator. Land-cheap
   works; expand-on-usage has no motor.
2. **Their own text says the solo buyer does not climb the ladder:** *"Most
   solo users find 20K–40K plenty for a month."* **OBSERVED, from the seller,
   against their own upsell.** That is the strongest single line against
   transfer and it did not come from us.
3. **A variable bill is a different product to this buyer.** Viktor sells
   teams with a software budget. An owner-led shop budgets one fixed number a
   month; *"$59, and sometimes more"* is worse to that buyer than $79 flat.
   This is the reason our metering must present as **usage, not a balance**,
   and must never hard-stop.
4. **`NOT OBSERVED`, and it matters:** nothing on their pages says the model
   *works* — no churn, no retention, no realised ARPU, no free-to-paid
   conversion. **We can observe that they run it. We cannot observe that it
   pays.** Anyone arguing "Viktor proves usage pricing works" is arguing from
   a pricing page.

**What DOES transfer, and I recommend importing it: no feature tiering on the
metered layer.** Every entry buyer gets every shipped platform capability; the
only difference between plans is the allowance. It is a page a one-person
business can read in five seconds, it removes the feature-checklist comparison
we have been told not to fight, and it is fully consistent with the ruled
split — *the platform is metered, the employee is not* becomes *you get all of
the platform, you just get so much of it.*

**What to refuse:** metering the employee (ruled), and a credit *unit*. A
credit is a promise about a unit cost and item 24 is unbuilt. **Denominate the
allowance in things the owner recognises — images and videos — not in
credits.** That also dodges the register problem: "12 videos a month" is a
package; "20,000 credits" is a meter.
