# ROUND 1 — GATE A: SKEPTICAL SMB OWNER (verbatim)

*Reviewer persona: Dana R., 51, two-location salon, six chairs.*

## VERDICT: **FAIL** (round 1)

Not because the thinking is bad. The thinking is better than most of what gets put in front of me. It fails because of what it hands the copy people at the end, and because the file breaks its own rules in the exact two places that go on a page.

Six gate-blocking findings, in order of how bad:

1. **§1.6's "durable" hero and §6.1's position paragraph both promise things that do not exist, with no "coming" label** — which is precisely what §2.4 forbids ("Every layer-2 sentence is labelled as coming, with no date, or it does not appear") and what §6.2 bars ("Anything implying the employee is available today"). §6 is titled *"what the copy orgs may write tomorrow."* You wrote your own violation into the cascade section.
2. **"One free thing the frame gives us that nobody has claimed yet"** (§1.6, on cancel/fire) — your own Holo snapshot prints *"Cancel anytime"* in the pricing header and *"24/7 cancel, no lock-in"* on the same page (`2026-09-03-holo-primary.md` §1, OBSERVED). A claim contradicted by a receipt in your own folder. Law 2.
3. **"your data leaves with you" is recommended for the hero block** (§1.6). Your objection bank says the opposite in brackets: *"if you leave, your content and calendar go with you [TRUE ONLY AFTER P-C SHIPS — do not say until then]"* and *"NO export/portability commitment exists anywhere in the corpus."* Two artifacts disagreeing on a shared fact. Law 4 fails both.
4. **The allowance instruction contradicts a ruling you already have.** §4.1 says N and M stay blank; §6.2 bars *"any allowance number until item 24."* Objection bank O3 grades the $59 sentence **RECEIPTED as of D2 (Phin, 2 Sep)** with actual numbers: *"a planned week of posts every week, drafted for your approval, images included, four videos a month."* You have just re-blocked the one sentence Phin already ruled. And separately, `ENTRY_WEEKLY_ALLOWANCE = {video: 4}` is **weekly** — the bank says **monthly**. Somebody has to tell me which, and it is not going to be me.
5. **Buyer-psychology claims stated flat, with no receipt and no `UNTESTED`.** §4.4 *"Easier, and for a reason the chief did not name"*; §4.4 *"A shop owner hitting a wall at 11pm is churn"*; §5.3 *"'$59, and sometimes more' is worse to that buyer than $79 flat."* Nobody asked me. Those are three people in a room deciding what I feel. Law 1 says every untested assumption is labeled inline, everywhere. These are not.
6. **§1's own heading: "THE HIRING FRAME — tested as a strong candidate, and adopted."** Nothing was tested. Your own second paragraph says zero owners have seen a line of this. That word in a heading is the pink button.

Plus one thing that is going to cause an actual accident: **§6 says "Two columns, and the right one is the only one that may ship."** The right-hand column is headed **May NOT say**. Somebody on a copy team reading fast is going to put "Every channel" on a landing page.

Is somebody counting something? What happens when it runs out? You put the scary word in and then didn't explain it. That is the sentence where I close the tab.

**What do I think this company sells?** Something that posts on Instagram for me and texts me a lot. I am about 70% sure it's an app. The word "employee" makes me think maybe there's a person, and then the word "It" three words later says no. So I'm confused within one sentence.

**What do I think it costs?** Forty dollars? Because everything like this is forty dollars. If you told me $299 I would laugh, and I want you to know I would laugh *before* you got to explain, which I think is the whole problem you spent nine pages on.

**Would I click?** No. Not on the durable one. That's the real finding here: **your placeholder copy is better than your position.**
