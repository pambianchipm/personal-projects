## Overview

The PM deck's own framing (p38, verbatim): *"Sometimes the biggest
competitor is inertia. The most likely alternative? Do nothing."*

For Player 2, INACTION is not an owner who has decided against
marketing. It is the far more common owner who has decided **for** it,
repeatedly, and has not sustained it — a pattern, not a verdict on them.
The product is not sold against apathy; it is sold against a specific
pattern: enthusiasm → batch → drift → silence → re-enthusiasm. The
*cadence* of that pattern is sourced (below); the feelings once written
into it — guilt in particular — are `UNTESTED` and have been taken back
out. Which feeling actually sits in the silence is a first-order
interview question.

This is the #1 alternative by volume. That every other dossier's
prospect is coming from it is `UNTESTED` — there are no prospects yet,
and this org has never spoken to one.

## SWOT (of inaction, from the owner's seat)

**Strengths**
- Free, in cash. No decision, no vendor, no login, no contract.
- Zero trust cost. Nobody else touches the brand's voice or the accounts.
- Infinitely flexible — a good week gets three posts, a bad week gets none,
  and nothing breaks.
- Authenticity is automatic. The owner's own phone, own words, own face.
  Nothing on the market beats this on believability, and Player 2 should
  never pretend otherwise.

**Weaknesses**
- It doesn't compound. The 8–15% reach figure above is only collectable by
  an account that posts; the drift pattern means the owner pays the effort
  cost of starting repeatedly and collects the compounding benefit never.
- The loop never closes. The landscape report's emotional-core finding:
  the job's defining texture is **"the impossibility of closing the
  loop"** — the owner cannot tell which post did anything, so effort
  feels unrewarded, which **we infer** causes the fade. `UNTESTED` — see
  NOT CHECKED (i); the fade is sourced, the cause is not.
- Engagement is the step that never batches (landscape §1.8). Whatever the
  owner batches on Sunday, the comments and DMs still arrive Wednesday.
- Invisible cost. Owner hours are unpriced, so the alternative *looks*
  free at the exact moment it is most expensive.

**Opportunities (for us)**
- The fade is predictable and self-diagnosed. Owners describe it in their
  own words unprompted — which makes it the cheapest thing in the world to
  sell against IF the sentence is theirs, not ours. `UNTESTED`.
- Whether guilt, fatigue, or plain indifference is the dominant feeling
  behind inaction is `UNTESTED` — and the three differ completely in
  what they would buy. **Do not open on guilt.** *(Gate r1 killed the
  original bullet, "Guilt is already present. We are not creating a
  need; we are naming one." The gate persona's stated objection (a
  persona, not a customer — not evidence): that guilt is the wrong
  premise, and fatigue and indifference would buy differently. Full
  text in the shift-12 verdict.)*
- The "put the phone down on Saturday" line (landscape report's Appendix A
  synthesis) is, per that report, plausibly the strongest sentence this
  company can say. **It is a hypothesis about resonance, not a finding.**

**Threats (to us)**
- INACTION costs $0 and has no switching cost. Against **A1 $59 entry /
  A2 $299 founding / A3 $499 standard**, the owner is not comparing
  features; they are comparing our price against a number they believe
  is zero. The gap is not one number — at A3 it is roughly eight times
  the entry figure this bullet was originally written against, and
  whether the objection scales with it is `UNTESTED`. *(A3 sync, gate
  r2: the battle cards had been given all three prices and this dossier
  was still reasoning off $59 alone — law 4 between two artifacts filed
  the same shift.)*
- It cannot be "lost" — it is always available, and re-defaulting to it is
  what churn IS for this segment.
- Its authenticity advantage is real and permanent. Any Player 2 output
  that reads as generated hands the win straight back.
- **Our own honest reporting is a weapon against us here.** A month where
  the claims engine publishes nothing (n floors, FDR correction) reads to
  an owner exactly like the loop they already couldn't close.

## KSPs (what inaction actually "sells")

1. **It's free** — and the cost that makes it not free is invisible.
2. **It's yours** — your voice, your phone, no one to trust.
3. **It has no failure mode you can be blamed for.** Nothing was promised,
   so nothing is broken.
4. **It's already installed.** Zero onboarding, ever.

## Primary "features"

Meta Business Suite (free), the phone camera (sunk cost), an occasional
Canva session, and memory. That is the entire stack, and for a real share
of owners it is genuinely sufficient in a good month.

## What's great about it

It is honest. Nothing is exaggerated, nothing is automated into
blandness, and the owner never has to explain to a customer why their
Instagram sounds like someone else. Any pitch that sneers at this loses
the room. **The right posture is: treat inaction as rational, not as a
character flaw.** *Why* it is rational — our hypothesis is a loop that
never closed — is `UNTESTED`; see NOT CHECKED (i). The posture does not
depend on the cause being right. *(Fourth causal site, labelled at gate
r2: three had been labelled and this one missed — and this is the one
that tells a salesperson what to believe about the owner's inner life
before they walk in. Law 1 says everywhere it appears.)*

## Who should pick it (said plainly, and we should mean it)

- An owner whose customers do not come from social at all (pure trade
  referral, pure walk-by, B2B contract work).
- An owner in a genuinely seasonal trough with no offer to make.
- An owner who posts consistently *already* and enjoys it. If the ritual
  is working, we are selling a solution to a solved problem.
- An owner who cannot supply any raw material — no photos, no dates, no
  offers. Player 2 needs inputs; an owner who will not open the app is
  buying a subscription to a silent employee. **This is the honest
  disqualification and it belongs in the battle card.**

## The uncomfortable finding, recorded

The strongest argument for INACTION is one we produced ourselves. The
truth-map's headline (`positioning-truth-map.md` §2) is that the
employee's **voice** is thin: almost every shipped surface is *pull* —
the owner must open the console. An owner who defaults to inaction
because they never open things is, structurally, an owner who may
default to inaction *inside our product*. Gap 1 (initiative) is
therefore not a nice-to-have for this competitor; it is the
competitive answer to it. Product ask, not a claim.
