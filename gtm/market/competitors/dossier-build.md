## Overview

The PM deck (p38, verbatim): *"Often an alternative is to build a
product internally. One component of your value prop may be why it's
better to buy (or license) than create from scratch."*

For an SMB there is no engineering team, so "build" means **assemble**:
Canva for the graphic, ChatGPT for the caption, CapCut for the Reel, a
free scheduler for the posting, and — the part that decides everything —
a person to run it. Usually the owner. Sometimes a part-time hire, a
front-desk employee with "good Instagram," or the nephew.

This is the most *respectable* alternative. It is what a competent,
cost-conscious operator does, and it produces real results while the
person running it keeps running it.

## SWOT (of the assembled stack)

**Strengths**
- Cheap in cash and genuinely capable. Canva + CapCut + ChatGPT in
  competent hands produces work that is not obviously worse than ours.
- Total control and instant iteration. No approval queue, no vendor.
- No trust transfer. The owner keeps the passwords and the voice.
- Modular — swap any part for free, any week.

**Weaknesses**
- **The 4% number is the whole dossier — with its population named.**
  46% use AI to have ideas; 4% use it to plan the calendar. The
  assembled stack automates the *making* and leaves the *running* — the
  deciding, the sequencing, the remembering, the showing up — entirely
  on a human. That human is the bottleneck, and the bottleneck is the
  product. **`UNTESTED` as applied to our buyer:** the poll measured
  professional SMMs, and the same report says of the owner ICP that
  "they barely buy tools." The *shape* of the finding (AI drafts, humans
  plan) is what we carry; the percentage is not an owner statistic and
  must never be quoted to an owner as one. *(Caveat added at the
  competitor-salesperson gate r1, which called this "the single most
  load-bearing statistic in this dossier, measured on a population that
  is not the buyer.")*
- Nothing in the stack closes the loop. No attribution, no learning: next
  month starts from the same blank page as this month.
- Engagement still never batches (landscape §1.8).
- Failure is silent. A scheduler that drops a post tells nobody.
- Key-person risk. The nephew goes to college; the front-desk hire
  leaves; the owner gets busy. The stack survives, the output stops.
- Fragmentation cost is real but invisible: five tools, five logins, five
  places for the thread to break.

**Opportunities (for us)**
- We are not competing with the tools — we should say so. The pitch is
  "we replace the *person operating them*," which is a claim the 4%
  statistic supports directly and which nothing in the stack contests.
- Our failure alerting is a named market gap: `notify.ts`'s own header
  says "the market's #1 complaint about every competitor is silent
  publishing failures; we alert proactively instead."
- Our approvals model preserves the control the DIY buyer prizes —
  nothing posts or spends without the owner's click. That is the same
  value the DIY stack is bought for, kept.

**Threats (to us)**
- The stack gets better every quarter for free, and its vendors have
  vastly more distribution than we do.
- Canva and CapCut both ship AI features that narrow the making gap; if
  our differentiation is ever "better assets," we lose.
- Cash-cost anchoring: $28–55 vs A1's $59 is a fair fight only if the
  labour number is credible to the buyer, and its source is one
  directional citation. **If a prospect challenges $500–1,200/mo, we do
  not have a second source. Do not lead with it.**
- The nephew is free and family. That is a price and a relationship we
  cannot beat on either axis.

## KSPs (what BUILD sells)

1. **Cheap and known.** Every tool is one the owner has already heard of.
2. **You stay in control of everything.**
3. **Best-in-class parts.** Canva really is the best design layer for
   this buyer; CapCut really does dominate short-form.
4. **No commitment.** Cancel any piece, any month.

## Primary features

Design (Canva) · short-form video + auto-captions (CapCut) · copy and
ideation (ChatGPT) · scheduling (Meta Business Suite free, or Buffer) ·
link-in-bio (Linktree) · measurement (native Insights, glanced at).

## What's great about it

It is honest about what it is: tools for a person. Nothing in the stack
claims to run your marketing, so nothing in the stack disappoints you by
failing to. And in the hands of an owner who enjoys this work, it is
excellent — better than us on authenticity, equal on assets, and
$0–40/mo cheaper.

## Who should pick it

- An owner who **enjoys** making content and has the hours. This is a
  real and non-trivial share of the segment, and selling them a marketing
  employee is selling a nanny to someone who likes their kids.
- A business with a genuine in-house creative (a stylist who films well,
  a chef who shoots the plates) — the raw material advantage is already
  captured and the marginal value of Player 2 is only in the operating
  layer.
- Anyone below roughly $59/mo of felt pain. If the marketing genuinely
  isn't costing them customers, the stack is right.
- An owner whose main need is *assets*, not *operations*. Say it plainly
  and lose the deal cleanly; a mis-sold operations subscription churns.

## The trade route (product asks, for Phin then the chief → build org)

1. **The 4% gap is the wedge and it should be a demo, not a sentence.**
   The one thing the stack cannot do is show a planned month that
   maintains itself. Whatever surface demonstrates that fastest is the
   highest-leverage demo asset we have. *(Proposal, not a build order.)*
2. **Import, don't replace.** An owner with two years of Canva brand kit
   and a CapCut habit is not going to abandon them. `UNTESTED` whether
   "bring your own assets" is a purchase-unblocking feature — but it is
   the single most obvious BUILD-specific objection and we currently have
   no receipt either way. Candidate interview question.
