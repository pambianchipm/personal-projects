# ROUND 1 — GATE A: SKEPTICAL SMB OWNER (verbatim)

*Reviewer persona: Dana R., 51, two-location salon, six chairs.*

## VERDICT: **FAIL** (round 1)

Not because the thinking is bad. The thinking is better than most of what gets put in front of me. It fails because of what it hands the copy people at the end, and because the file breaks its own rules in the exact two places that go on a page.

Six gate-blocking findings, in order of how bad:

1. **§1.6's "durable" hero and §6.1's position paragraph both promise things that do not exist, with no "coming" label** — which is precisely what §2.4 forbids ("Every layer-2 sentence is labelled as coming, with no date, or it does not appear") and what §6.2 bars ("Anything implying the employee is available today"). §6 is titled *"what the copy orgs may write tomorrow."* You wrote your own violation into the cascade section.
2. **"One free thing the frame gives us that nobody has claimed yet"** (§1.6, on cancel/fire) — your own Holo snapshot prints *"Cancel anytime"* in the pricing header and *"24/7 cancel, no lock-in"* on the same page (`2026-09-03-holo-primary.md` §1, OBSERVED). A claim contradicted by a receipt in your own folder. Law 2.
3. **"your data leaves with you" is recommended for the hero block** (§1.6). Your objection bank says the opposite in brackets: *"if you leave, your content and calendar go with you [TRUE ONLY AFTER P-C SHIPS — do not say until then]"* and *"NO export/portability commitment exists anywhere in the corpus."* Two artifacts disagreeing on a shared fact. Law 4 fails both.
4. **The allowance instruction contradicts a ruling you already have.** §4.1 says N and M stay blank; §6.2 bars *"any allowance number until item 24."* Objection bank O3 grades the $59 sentence **RECEIPTED as of D2 (Phin, 2 Sep)** with actual numbers: *"a planned week of posts every week, drafted for your approval, images included, four videos a month."* You have just re-blocked the one sentence Phin already ruled. And separately, `ENTRY_WEEKLY_ALLOWANCE = {video: 4}` is **weekly** — the bank says **monthly**. Somebody has to tell me which, and it is not going to be me.
5. **Buyer-psychology claims stated flat, with no receipt and no `UNTESTED`.** §4.4 *"Easier, and for a reason the chief did not name"*; §4.4 *"A shop owner hitting a wall at 11pm is churn"*; §5.3 *"'$59, and sometimes more' is worse to that buyer than $79 flat."* Nobody asked me. Those are three people in a room deciding what I feel. Law 1 says every untested assumption is labeled inline, everywhere. These are not.
6. **§1's own heading: "THE HIRING FRAME — tested as a strong candidate, and adopted."** Nothing was tested. Your own second paragraph says zero owners have seen a line of this. That word in a heading is the pink button.

Plus one thing that is going to cause an actual accident: **§6 says "Two columns, and the right one is the only one that may ship."** The right-hand column is headed **May NOT say**. Somebody on a copy team reading fast is going to put "Every channel" on a landing page.

"Hire Player 2. The marketing employee for your shop. It works where you already text, asks you what actually sold, and hands back finished posts."

**§6.1, unedited:**

> Same thing, longer, and now it ends with "The platform is metered. The employee never is." I have read that sentence four times and I still do not know what it means. Metered like my water bill? Is somebody counting something? What happens when it runs out? You put the scary word in and then didn't explain it. That is the sentence where I close the tab.

**What do I think this company sells?** Something that posts on Instagram for me and texts me a lot. I am about 70% sure it's an app. The word "employee" makes me think maybe there's a person, and then the word "It" three words later says no. So I'm confused within one sentence.

**What do I think it costs?** Forty dollars? Because everything like this is forty dollars. If you told me $299 I would laugh, and I want you to know I would laugh *before* you got to explain, which I think is the whole problem you spent nine pages on.

**Would I click?** No. Not on the durable one. I'd maybe click the "today" version — *"writes your posts, publishes them to Instagram and Facebook, runs your Meta ads, and tells you what worked"* — because every single thing in it is a thing I can picture. That's the real finding here: **your placeholder copy is better than your position.**

## THE OBJECTIONS THE ARTIFACT CANNOT ANSWER

### 1. "You're charging me $59 a month to do data entry for your software." (the standing one)

**What the artifact says back** — §2.2:
> *"The asking is how we get the other half, and it is the honest answer to the SMB gate's standing objection ('your card charges me $299 to do data entry for software'): a one-line reply to a colleague is not data entry."*

**Does it hold? No.** It restates my objection and then contradicts it with an analogy. Three things are missing and each one alone sinks it:

- **Frequency.** Nowhere in this file — not §2.2, not §6.3, not the guardrails — does anyone say how often it asks. Once a week? Every post? Every day at 8am? I have six chairs and I am standing behind one of them. The single number that decides whether this is a colleague or a nag **does not appear in the document.** That absence is the finding.
- **What happens if I don't answer.** Also absent. Does the loop stop? Does it guess? Does it nag again? §2.2 says the asking *"is the attribution mechanism"* — so if I go quiet for three weeks in December, does the product's core value quietly stop working while I keep paying?
- **The analogy is doing the arguing.** "A colleague asking" and "a phone notification asking" are not the same thing, and you know it, because a colleague can see I'm mid-color and wait. Your own objection bank grades effort (O17) as **PARTIAL** and says involvement *"should be DEMONSTRATED (taps per week), not asserted."* §2.2 asserts it. That's the org's own standard, failed inside the org's own file.

**The honest version, which the file could have written:** it asks me a yes/no question once a week, it never asks twice, and if I ignore it, here is exactly what degrades. None of those three facts exist yet. Until they do, "it asks you things" cannot lead.

### 2. "Is 'asking me questions' actually a benefit, or is it another thing wanting me?"

Straight answer from a salon owner: **it is an annoyance that becomes a benefit only if it is cheap for me and visibly pays me back.** Right now the file has it exactly backwards.

The four properties, ranked the way I actually experience them:

1. **"You send a phone clip; it comes back cut and captioned."** — Pure gain. I have four hundred unposted videos. This is the whole product.
2. **"No app to open."** — Pure gain. Four words, and it's the relief the whole file is looking for.
3. **"It lives where you already are."** — Neutral. Depends entirely on #4.
4. **"It asks you things."** — **A cost.** Every time, at the moment it happens.

§2.2 puts my #4 at its #1, and defends it with *"This is the property to lead with, because it is the only one of the four that is simultaneously (a) structural, (b) unclaimed by either comparable, and (c) load-bearing."* Read those three reasons again. **All three are reasons it's good for you.** None is a reason it's good for me. That's Law 6 — you are not your customer — happening in the middle of a section that thinks it's being rigorous.

The asking only converts from cost to benefit if the *answer comes back to me*: "You said Tuesday's discount was busy. I'm putting more behind that one." That sentence — the payback — **appears nowhere in this document.** Without it, you are asking a woman with a phone full of unread apps to file weekly reports with her marketing software.

### 3. "Wait — 'nothing gets spent.' Whose money, and how much?"

The best line in the file raises a question the file never answers. Ad spend is mentioned constantly — *"runs your Meta ads,"* *"spends real ad money under a ceiling"* — and **the document never once says the ad budget is mine, separate from the subscription, and set by me.** §3.2's spend ceiling is UNBUILT and correctly withheld. So the strongest hero line in the file opens a hole where my wallet is, with nothing behind it. For a $59 buyer, "and also you'll be spending on ads" is a bigger number than $59, and it is invisible here.

### 4. "Will my clients know a robot wrote it?"

§6.2 says I may be told *"It's an AI, and it always says so."* Says so **to whom**? Your objection bank, O5, is the clearest thing in this repo on the point:

> *"NO artifact answers whether the PUBLISHED post or DM reply discloses AI authorship to the public."* Grade: **NONE-YET (policy undecided).**

If a client DMs my salon at 9pm and a machine answers as my salon, I need to know today whether it says so, because that's my name on the door and my regulars are the whole business. The artifact promotes an unruled policy into the sayable column. That's a gate-fail on its own.

### 5. "What do I actually get, this month, for $59?"

§4.1's recommended copy is *"$59/mo, including N image generations and M videos a month."* You cannot sell a blank. And you didn't have to — objection bank O3 records that Phin **already ruled the sentence on 2 September**: *"a planned week of posts every week, drafted for your approval, images included, four videos a month,"* plus a 30-day refund. That's a sentence I'd read and understand. This file bars it. Then `ENTRY_WEEKLY_ALLOWANCE = {video: 4}` says *four a week*, which is four times as much. **Two of your files disagree about the only number I'd read.** Under Law 4 that fails both.

### 6. "The last person who took my money gave me a folder of graphics."
