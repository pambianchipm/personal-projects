## VERDICT: FAIL

I would give these people my email — for the free scan, because "three posts in your voice, before you pay anything, no card" is a clean offer and I can judge the posts myself in two minutes. So it's not a trust FAIL on the whole page. It fails because the biggest type on the page tells me I can hire something that does not exist, and the correction is sitting in small grey type a band lower where people like me never get to. I have been sold that exact shape before. And the one line in the hero that would actually make me buy — "tells you which ones moved units" — is the one line the product can't do on its own. Fix those two and the price hole and I'd call it honest. As written, the page is trading on a skim.

## MY ANSWERS

**1.** Five seconds: kicker says YOUR NEXT HIRE, headline says "Come hire Player2," sub tells me it writes my week, posts to Instagram and Facebook, runs my Meta ads. I thought: this is a robot that does my social, it's live, there's a free trial thing, I can start tonight. I was wrong. It doesn't exist. Nothing in the hero told me that. The button even says "Start with a free scan," which made me more sure it was real and buyable — and the odd thing is the scan IS real, so the one true button is what sold me the false idea.

**2.** `Let it read your public pages and write you three posts. Then decide.` That's the whole thing. No card, no meeting, no signup call, I look at three posts and I know in thirty seconds whether these people can write like a bakery. That's the only sentence on the page that costs me nothing and shows me something.

**3.** `Come hire Player2.` Because after I read further and found out it isn't built, that line stops being a headline and becomes the reason I don't trust the rest. Second place, and it's close: `Get in line` — a line to what, and who tells me when it's my turn?

**4.** Yes. First read, no hesitation: it exists, it's for sale, "hire" means hire. "YOUR NEXT HIRE" made it worse, not better — "next hire" is a thing I do next week when I post the job, not a thing that hasn't been invented. I did not think "next" meant "someday." Nobody does.

**5.** "Come hire it before it's built." My honest read: I get in early, I'm ahead of other shops, and when it's ready I'm near the front. That's what "before" does — it implies there's an after, and I'm in it. Do I think I've reserved one? Yes, that's exactly what I think. And then I find out there's no date, no price, and — reading the small type — nobody is going to contact me either. So what I think I'm being promised is a place in a queue and a heads-up when it opens. As far as I can tell from these words, I'm being promised neither. That's the gap that would make me angry.

**6.** "Moved units" means it tells me which post sold croissants. That's the whole dream — I've never once known whether a post did anything for the till. Now you tell me it only knows if I log the sales myself. Yes, I feel misled, and I'll tell you exactly why: I don't log sales. Nobody with eleven staff at 6am logs sales against Instagram posts. So the feature I got excited about is a feature I will never actually have, and I would only find that out after I'd paid. The honest version — "it tells you what people reacted to, and if you log sales it'll tie those to posts" — is still worth something to me. Say that.

**7.** No, I did not get it in one reading. I got it on the third. Where I fell over: "or once, in advance, for the posts you set to go out on their own." I read that four times. Once what? Click once? And "in advance" of what — I'm setting posts to go out on their own, which is the thing I thought "nothing posts without your click" just said couldn't happen. So the first half of the sentence takes away what the first six words gave me, and it does it inside a comma sandwich. Then "Ads are never set in advance" — fine, that part is clear and I like it. Could I explain it back to my partner? I could explain the ads half. I could not explain the posting half. I'd say "I think you approve them somehow, in a batch, maybe?" and she'd say "so does it post without us or not," and I would not know.

**8.** Three. `No app to open.` — I never said I minded apps; now I'm wondering why they think I do. `We're not taking money for it.` — I hadn't offered any, and telling me you're not taking my money makes me check my pockets. And the entire second half of the safety band about ad budgets is answering a fear about runaway ad spend that I hadn't got to yet — though I'll grant that one is at least a fear I'd have had eventually.

**9.** A lie, no. Two things I'd be angry about in three months: (a) "moved units" — because I'd have bought it for that; (b) "Come hire Player2" over a thing that isn't built, because when I find out I'll remember that I was made to find out, rather than told. And one I'd be angry about in three weeks: I typed my email into "Get in line" and heard nothing, and I have no idea whether I'm in the line, whether there is a line, or whether anyone will ever write to me.

**10.** Price. It's just not here. The old page apparently said $59 a month; this one says nothing, and "we're not taking money for it" is not a price, it's a dodge dressed up as honesty. Second: what happens after I type my email — who contacts me, how, and roughly when, even if the answer is "we can't email you yet, check back." Third: how many posts is "a week"? Four? Seven? I need a number to know if this is worth anything. Fourth: does it only do Instagram and Facebook, forever or for now — because half my under-30 customers are on TikTok and if the answer is no, I want to be told no on the page, not in month four.

## MUST-FIX

**1. `YOUR NEXT HIRE` / `Come hire Player2.` (hero)**
For a reader this says: available, purchasable, today. The correction lives a whole band down in small type, and the page's own CTA ("Start with a free scan") reinforces the wrong reading because that part IS live. This is the pattern that gets people burned.
What I'd rather: put it in the big type. `YOUR NEXT HIRE · STILL BEING BUILT` as the kicker, headline unchanged. What my version promises: an employee is being built and I can register interest. What it does NOT promise: a date, a price, a place in a queue, or that I can have it now. I'd avoid "JOINING SOON" — "soon" is a date, and you say you don't have one.

**2. `and tells you which ones moved units.` (hero sub)**
Reader hears: it knows what sold. Product does it only if I log my own sales, which I won't. This is the single most persuasive clause on the page and it's the one that isn't true for me by default.
What I'd rather: `…and tells you which ones people reacted to — and which ones sold, if you log your sales.` What that promises: likes, comments and shares, automatically. What it does NOT promise: any sales tracking without me typing sales in. It's a weaker line and it'll cost you some signups. It'll cost you fewer refunds.

**3. `Get in line` + the deleted "we'll come to you when it's ready."**
You cut the only sentence that told me what happens after I hand over my email, and put nothing in its place. So the form asks for something and answers nothing. For a reader that's worse than the promise you were worried about.
What I'd rather: say the true thing under the button, whatever it is. If invites go out from the site and not by email, say `We'll open invites here — check back. We're not set up to email you yet.` What that promises: nothing arrives in my inbox. What it does NOT promise: a place, an order, a date, or a founding rate. If you genuinely cannot say anything true about what happens next, then don't collect the email — send me to the free scan instead, which is a thing you can actually deliver.

**4. The safety band, one sentence.**
I did not understand it on one reading and I could not explain the posting half back. The clause "or once, in advance, for the posts you set to go out on their own" is where it breaks — it appears to give back the thing the sentence just took away.
What I'd rather: two sentences, posting and ads kept apart, and say plainly whether scheduled posts go out without me. Something like: `Nothing posts and nothing spends without your click. You can approve posts one at a time, or approve a batch up front and let those go out on schedule. Ads are different: every campaign waits for its own click, on a button showing the budget.` What that promises: I approve everything at some point before it happens. What it does NOT promise: that I see each post at the moment it goes out. If "batch approve up front" isn't what the product does, then I've got it wrong — which is my point, because that's what the current sentence made me guess.

**5. No price anywhere.**
The live page had a number; this copy drops it. I will not queue for a thing whose cost I don't know, and "we're not taking money for it" reads as evasion, not generosity. I don't need a final price. I need a shape — "expect around $X a month" or "founding shops from $X."
I can't write your price line for you. But shipping the page with no number at all is a choice I'd notice and hold against you.

## SHOULD-FIX

**1.** The hero sub and `It writes. It posts. It runs the ads. It tells you what worked.` are the same four verbs twice on one screen. Second time I read it I stopped believing it was a list and started thinking it was padding. Cut one. Given fix #2 above, cut the Platform title version and let the hero carry it.

**2.** `the chat apps you already use` — name one. WhatsApp? Texts? Messenger? Unnamed, it reads like a thing you haven't decided yet, which (fair enough) it is. Name the one you're actually building, or say "the apps you already use — we'll start with X."

**3.** The free scan needs my email before it runs (your own note says the button's dead until a URL and an email are typed), but every string about it says "before you pay anything / no card" and none of them says "we'll need your email." Say it up front. I hate finding out at the form.

**4.** `No app to open.` and `We're not taking money for it.` are both answering things I hadn't asked. The second one especially makes me pat my pockets. `The employee isn't built yet. No date, because we don't have one.` is honest and does the job on its own — that pair is the best writing on the page. Let it stand alone.

**5.** Nowhere does it say Instagram and Facebook are the only channels. It just never mentions others. A skimmer fills that in with TikTok, because that's where his customers are. Say it: `Instagram and Facebook today. Nothing else yet.` Better a no on the page than a no in month four.

**6.** No number for "a week." "The week's posts," "it brings back a week" — how many? Give me a figure or a range. Without one I assume it's small and you're hiding it.

**7.** `Watch it work a week` — is that a canned demo, or is it my shop? I read it as my shop for a second and got interested for the wrong reason. If it's a demo, say `Watch a demo week.`

**8.** Your meta description says `Not built yet: get in line.` That is more honest than your headline. When the thing in the search result is straighter than the thing on the page, the page is the problem.

**9.** `Who's writing your posts at 11pm?` — leave that exactly as it is. That's me. That's the only line here written by someone who has met me.

## WHAT I CANNOT JUDGE

Whether the posts are any good. That's the entire decision and no sentence on a page can settle it — which is why the free scan is your best asset and should probably be the loudest thing on the page, not the second thing. I also can't tell from words: whether it actually sounds like my bakery or like a bakery in general; whether "publishes to Instagram" means it works every time or works when Meta's in a good mood; whether the ads are competent or just spend my money in a well-gated way; whether there'll be a company here in a year to answer the phone; what you do with my public pages and my email after you scan them; and whether the person who wrote "Who's writing your posts at 11pm?" is the same person who'll be writing my posts, or whether that was a copywriter you hired once. None of that is your copy's fault. It just means the page can only get me to the scan — so stop trying to make it get me to a hire.
